Design principle. The 21 items are not treated as 21 unrelated puzzles. Each problem is a controlled transition: state → uncertainty → information → strategy → expectation → institution → system.

The 21-problem map

01

Gambler's Problem

مسئله قمارباز
Dynamic Programming

What stake maximizes the probability of reaching a target before ruin?

Mechanism: Bellman recursion over capital states; optimize the probability of hitting a terminal target.

Economic bridge: Capital allocation, bankroll management, financing under constraints.

02

Gambler's Ruin

ورشکستگی قمارباز
Probability / Risk

How does starting capital change the probability of eventual bankruptcy?

Mechanism: For fair play, ruin probability is determined by relative starting capital; finite bankrolls imply eventual absorption.

Economic bridge: Liquidity, competitive attrition, reserves, small-firm fragility.

03

St. Petersburg Paradox

پارادوکس سن‌پترزبورگ
Expected Value / Utility

Why does infinite expected monetary value conflict with ordinary willingness to pay?

Mechanism: The probability-weighted payoffs sum without bound; utility and practical constraints challenge a literal expected-value prescription.

Economic bridge: Fat tails, valuation, insurance, risk premia.

04

Martingale Trap

دام مارتینگل
Betting Strategy / Tail Risk

Can doubling after losses guarantee profit?

Mechanism: Geometric bet growth meets finite bankrolls, table limits and negative/zero expected games.

Economic bridge: Leverage, averaging down, debt-financed strategies.

05

Monty Hall

مسئله مونتی هال
Conditional Probability

Does switching after an informed reveal improve the chance of winning?

Mechanism: Under the standard host rule, switching wins with probability 2/3.

Economic bridge: Bayesian updating, screening, signals, auctions.

06

Allais Paradox

پارادوکس آلای
Behavioral Decision Theory

Why can certainty be valued disproportionately across otherwise similar lotteries?

Mechanism: Observed preference patterns violate the Independence axiom of expected utility.

Economic bridge: Insurance, consumer choice, pricing under risk.

07

Ellsberg Paradox

پارادوکس الزبرگ
Ambiguity

Why prefer known probabilities to unknown probabilities?

Mechanism: Ambiguity aversion produces preferences incompatible with a single precise expected-probability model.

Economic bridge: New markets, investment, regulation, geopolitical uncertainty.

08

Kelly Criterion

معیار کلی
Optimal Growth

What fraction of wealth should be risked when a repeated gamble has an edge?

Mechanism: Maximize expected logarithmic wealth growth rather than expected wealth per bet.

Economic bridge: Portfolio sizing, capital efficiency, long-run compounding.

09

Secretary / Best-Offer Problem

مسئله بهترین پیشنهاد
Optimal Stopping

When should you stop observing and commit to the best candidate seen?

Mechanism: A threshold strategy balances information gathering against the cost of waiting.

Economic bridge: Hiring, procurement, search markets, real-estate offers.

10

Goofspiel

بازی گوف‌اشپیل
Strategic Game

How should players bid when values are private and future information is strategically revealed?

Mechanism: Repeated zero-sum play with hidden cards creates state-dependent mixed strategies.

Economic bridge: Auctions, bidding, competitive pricing.

11

Pascal's Wager

شرط پاسکال
Infinite / Uncertain Utility

Can a tiny probability multiplied by an enormous payoff dominate a decision?

Mechanism: Expected-utility reasoning becomes unstable or controversial when utilities are infinite or unbounded.

Economic bridge: Existential risk, catastrophic policy, long-horizon investment.

12

Dutch Book

کتاب هلندی
Coherent Beliefs

Can inconsistent probability assessments be combined into a guaranteed loss?

Mechanism: A set of prices that violates probability coherence can be exploited by a suitable portfolio of bets.

Economic bridge: Market prices, forecasting, risk-neutral pricing.

13

Prisoner's Dilemma

معمای زندانی
Strategic Incentives

Why can individually rational choices produce a collectively worse outcome?

Mechanism: Dominant incentives conflict with joint welfare; repetition can change the strategic landscape.

Economic bridge: Cartels, competition, public goods, cooperation.

14

Keynesian Beauty Contest

مسابقه زیبایی کینزی
Strategic Expectations

Why can an asset's value depend on what others think others will value?

Mechanism: Higher-order beliefs turn prediction into a game about predictions.

Economic bridge: Asset pricing, bubbles, momentum, macro expectations.

15

Winner's Curse

نفرین برنده
Auctions / Information

Why can winning an auction be evidence that your estimate was too optimistic?

Mechanism: Conditional selection: the winner is disproportionately the bidder with the highest error.

Economic bridge: Procurement, M&A, spectrum auctions, resource valuation.

16

Gambler's Fallacy

مغالطه قمارباز
Probability / Cognition

Why do independent events feel as if they must compensate for previous outcomes?

Mechanism: Independence is confused with short-run balancing; the law of large numbers does not predict immediate reversal.

Economic bridge: Demand forecasting, trading signals, inventory decisions.

17

Hot-Hand Fallacy

مغالطه دست داغ
Probability / Cognition

When does apparent streakiness represent skill, and when is it random clustering?

Mechanism: Serial dependence must be distinguished from clustering that naturally occurs in independent sequences.

Economic bridge: Performance evaluation, sports markets, sales analytics.

18

Black Swan / Fat-Tail Problem

مسئله دنباله‌سنگین
Extreme Risk

What happens when rare events are much more consequential than the model assumes?

Mechanism: Thin-tail intuition can fail under heavy-tailed or model-uncertain distributions.

Economic bridge: Finance, supply chains, catastrophe planning.

19

Tragedy of the Commons

تراژدی منابع مشترک
Collective Action

Why can individually beneficial extraction destroy a shared resource?

Mechanism: Private marginal benefit is compared with a dispersed social cost.

Economic bridge: Fisheries, climate, congestion, common infrastructure.

20

Portfolio Diversification Dilemma

معمای تنوع‌بخشی
Portfolio / Risk

How should exposure be distributed when correlations and future regimes are uncertain?

Mechanism: Diversification reduces idiosyncratic risk but cannot eliminate common-factor or model risk.

Economic bridge: Investment, supply chains, product portfolios.

21

Red Queen / Survival Game

بازی ملکه سرخ
Evolutionary / Competitive Strategy

Why can rational adaptation leave every competitor running without improving relative position?

Mechanism: Payoffs depend on others' adaptation; equilibrium can require continuous costly improvement.

Economic bridge: Arms races, innovation, pricing competition, productivity races.

Research workflow

Inner-agent synthesis

  1. Mathematical lens: verify state space, probabilities, equations and solution concept.
  2. Decision lens: separate expected value, utility, risk and ambiguity.
  3. Strategic lens: identify incentives, information and equilibrium assumptions.
  4. Economic lens: translate the abstract mechanism into capital, markets, firms or institutions.
  5. Teaching lens: retain the simplest experiment that exposes the mechanism.

Outer-agent validation

  1. Source check: distinguish canonical historical facts from later interpretation.
  2. Cross-check: verify equations and named paradoxes against authoritative references.
  3. Scope check: avoid duplicate problems and preserve progression across the 21.
  4. Evidence labeling: mark canonical mechanism vs economic analogy.
  5. Revision loop: your critique becomes the next version's design specification.

What the sequence is trying to teach

The hidden subject is not gambling. Gambling is the laboratory because it strips decisions down to probability, payoff, information, incentives and repeated choice. The same structures then reappear in markets and economic institutions.

First transition
“How do I avoid ruin?” → survival and dynamic programming.
Second transition
“What is this gamble worth?” → expectation, utility, risk and ambiguity.
Third transition
“What should I do when others act?” → games, auctions and strategic expectations.
Fourth transition
“Can my model be wrong?” → cognition, tails and model uncertainty.
Fifth transition
“What happens when everyone optimizes?” → externalities, portfolios and competitive systems.

Evidence discipline: a mathematical puzzle, its historical origin, and its economic interpretation are three different claims. They should not be collapsed into a single “source”. The final research edition should attach a primary or authoritative reference to every row.

Recommended interactive evolution

  1. Play: let the user make the decision before revealing the canonical strategy.
  2. Simulate: run 100–10,000 repetitions and compare intuition with distribution.
  3. Explain: reveal the mathematical mechanism only after the experiment.
  4. Transfer: show the economic analogue beside the gambling problem.
  5. Stress-test: change bankroll, probabilities, information, payoff scale and horizon.
  6. Compare: put the user's strategy against the optimal/reference strategy.

Best next product form: an interactive “Decision Laboratory” where each problem has Experiment → Prediction → Play → Simulation → Mathematics → Economic analogue → Reflection.
